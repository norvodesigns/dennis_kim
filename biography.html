01 — Biography

Born in Korea. Raised in Canada. Educated in the United States.

A career that began with a solo debut at fourteen and has since crossed four continents.

Dennis Kim was born in Seoul in 1975 and moved to Toronto with his family at three months old. He began the violin at four, and from seven studied with the late Victor Danchenko at the Royal Conservatory of Music. At fourteen he made his solo debut with the Toronto Philharmonic, playing the Mendelssohn Violin Concerto in E minor.

He went on to the Curtis Institute of Music and the Yale School of Music. His principal teachers were Danchenko, Jaime Laredo, Aaron Rosand, Yumi Ninomiya Scott, Peter Oundjian and Paul Kantor; in chamber music he was coached by Isaac Stern, Felix Galimir and Claude Frank.

His first concertmaster appointment came at twenty-two, with the Tucson Symphony. Positions in Hong Kong, Seoul, Tampere and Buffalo followed, and in 2018 he was named Concertmaster of Pacific Symphony, where he holds the Eleanor and Michael Gordon Chair. He lives in Irvine, California with his wife and two daughters.

The early years

A start in Toronto.

He began the violin at four, not long after the family settled in Toronto, and from seven studied with the late Victor Danchenko at the Royal Conservatory of Music — the teacher who shaped his earliest years on the instrument.

Career

From the age of 22, Dennis Kim has held concertmaster posts on three continents and guest appearances on four.

Chronology

  1. 1975

    Born in Seoul

    Moves to Toronto with his family at three months old.

  2. Age 4

    Begins the violin

    Shortly after, an accident with a playground gate costs him the tip of a finger on his right hand. A skin graft follows, and a year away from practice. Had it been the left hand, he has said, he would almost certainly not be playing today.

  3. Age 7

    Royal Conservatory of Music

    Begins formal study in Toronto with the late Victor Danchenko.

  4. Age 14

    Solo debut

    Performs the Mendelssohn Violin Concerto in E minor with the Toronto Philharmonic.

  5. Curtis · Yale

    Conservatory years

    Studies with Jaime Laredo, Aaron Rosand and Yumi Ninomiya Scott at Curtis, and Peter Oundjian at Yale; also with Paul Kantor at the Aspen Music Festival.

  6. 1998

    A first appointment, at twenty-two

    Named concertmaster of the Tucson Symphony, then joins the Hong Kong Philharmonic as Third Associate Concertmaster.

  7. c. 2000

    Hong Kong Philharmonic

    Becomes concertmaster — the youngest in the orchestra's history.

  8. 2005

    Seoul Philharmonic

    Takes the concertmaster chair in Seoul.

  9. 2008

    Twenty Four Seasons in a week

    On ten hours' notice, steps in for an ailing William Preucil on the Busan Philharmonic's tour of Japan, performing Vivaldi's Four Seasons twenty times in seven days.

  10. 2010 — 2015

    Tampere Philharmonic

    The first foreign concertmaster in the Finnish orchestra's history.

  11. 2014

    Royal Albert Hall

    Guest concertmaster with the Bergen Philharmonic on a ten-city UK tour, and leads the Qatar Philharmonic's BBC Proms debut before an audience of eight thousand.

  12. 2015 — 2018

    Buffalo Philharmonic

    Concertmaster in New York, commuting to Toronto to teach at his alma mater, the Royal Conservatory of Music.

  13. 2018 — Present

    Pacific Symphony

    Named concertmaster in April, succeeding Raymond Kobler; begins in September and moves to Irvine, California.

  14. 2021 — Present

    Trio Barclay

    Co-founds the trio with cellist Jonah Kim and pianist Sean Kennard as the first Ensemble-in-Residence of the Irvine Barclay Theatre.

Off the stage

A second, entirely unofficial career in sport.

Written by Dennis Kim — and, he would like it noted, entirely in his own words. This section is a humorous personal aside, not a factual record.

From an early age Dennis showed incredible athletic ability. At five he was selected to play right field for his church baseball team, alongside seventeen-year-olds. He went 0-for-63 at the plate, but made only ten errors in eleven chances in the field.

He went on to play sweeper for his church soccer team, which allowed no goals across a five-game tournament. The goalkeeper — who made roughly two saves all week — was named MVP. His parents thought the award unfair and changed churches.

Through primary and junior school he was picked first overall for every recess sport: basketball, football, baseball, four square, wall ball. He was especially good at floor hockey, and had his parents been able to afford skates, he would surely have made the NHL.

In high school he played tight end and place kicker for the Northern Secondary School Red Knights, who won the city championship. He caught zero balls all season, but blocked opposing ends with skill and power, allowing zero sacks.

In college he held a two-year unbeaten streak in ping pong, having chosen his opponents wisely. Nicknamed weak style, he would begin matches with a different grip to keep things interesting, and frustrated opponents to painful defeat.

In Hong Kong he played left back for the Philharmonic's football team, where he was nicknamed the Great Wall — ironically, the only Korean in a side full of Chinese players. In Seoul he was converted to striker and made captain, scoring highlight-reel goals and taking full advantage of the fact that there was no offside.

A talented golfer, he perfected an unorthodox half swing without ever taking a lesson, shot a personal best of 77, recorded two eagles, and retired in 2004. In Finland he played football and hockey for his orchestra's teams and was drafted by the Tampere Tigers, a local semi-professional baseball club; the fixtures clashed with rehearsals, and he never suited up.

Since 2018, California has been the home base for his athletic domination, where he regularly crushes opponents at racquetball and pickleball. In the summer of 2022 he again went undefeated in ping pong at a festival full of self-proclaimed excellent ping pongers. Interlochen 2022 has been unofficially titled the Dennis Kim Ping Pong Summer.
